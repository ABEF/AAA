### Reward timing and maintenance schedule

Rewards do not accrue on a fixed per-minute clock. Supported venue fees become funded holder balances when harvested and allocated; creator payments become funded balances when their immutable inbox is processed with `syncExternal`. The 80/10/5/5 split applies to those actual receipts, with raw-unit rounding as documented in [economics](ECONOMICS.md). A creator's payment frequency is external to this keeper and remains undecided. No keeper can manufacture missing revenue.

The example `interval_seconds=600` checks for work every ten minutes. This is a polling interval, not a guaranteed transaction schedule. `maintenance.harvest_interval_seconds=14400` makes conventional harvesting eligible every four hours since its last confirmed keeper harvest; other actions and pending confirmations may delay it. External inbox processing is attempted when a scan finds a pending payment. Conventional fees are also harvested within relevant on-chain entry/maintenance operations.

Rebalancing is conditional on `Strategy.needsRebalance(bucketId)`: the bucket must be active, operations enabled, `minRebalanceInterval` elapsed, and the oracle-reference price must have moved by at least `triggerBps` from the recorded rebalance center. Execution additionally checks oracle freshness, spot deviation, slippage, minimum liquidity, deadlines, roles and pause state. Production `rangeBps`, `triggerBps`, `minRebalanceInterval` and execution bounds remain unset in the release inventory; the ten-minute polling example does not choose them. Rebalance once per shared pool, regardless of its NFT count. Compounding separately requires the configured raw-token reserve threshold. Backtesting and actual operating costs remain release gates; rebalancing does not eliminate impermanent loss.

Royalty payments use a separate clock and ledger. Plain admitted-asset receipts are recognized by `sync`, `syncAll`, activation or a claim; `claimable` includes pending receipts at the current eligible weights. A current owner can call `RoyaltyRewards.claim(id,asset)` whenever a funded entitlement exists; the claim synchronizes pending receipts automatically. LP claims use `Rewards.claim(id,token)` and require previously allocated funding: that claim does not harvest a venue or process the creator inbox. There is no time lock or periodic claim window for either reward ledger, and strategy pause does not pause claims. Each call covers one NFT and one asset. The public website still simulates LP claims and has no live royalty balance/claim integration.

### Optional metadata notifications

`metadata_refresh` is disabled in the example. To enable it on a reviewed canonical-chain test deployment, configure its exact `nft` address outside the existing `contracts` map and its reviewed runtime Keccak hash at `code_hashes.nft`. Keep the existing shared-pool deployment identity and pending records intact. The watcher verifies controller/NFT/Vault bindings, canonical chain, seal, immutable limits and ordinal-to-token identities at a pinned confirmed block. It rotates through at most 50 NFTs per scan, comparing evolution stage, valuation availability and trait readiness; this separate notification scan never rebalances individual NFTs.

`max_refresh_batch` bounds a notification to 50 inclusive mint ordinals. The watcher uses the existing simulation, serial signer, gas/daily budgets and durable pending-transaction recovery. A baseline advances only after a matching successful confirmed receipt, canonical observation/receipt anchors and bounded observation age. It rereads the exact NFTs at the receipt block and checkpoints that state rather than the earlier trigger. Dry runs, failed or ambiguous submissions, reorgs and expired observations do not count as notified; invalidated triggers remove previous range fingerprints to force a fresh attempt. Initial scans have no invented baseline. Changed-work cursors advance after the selected nonwrapping notification range so small batches and wraparound cannot starve trailing NFTs. Pool maintenance and metadata alternate priority, and metadata notifications can continue during strategy pause. Disabling new scans still reconciles an existing metadata transaction. The fingerprint covers stage, availability and reveal, not every within-stage USD-value change. Review logs, RPC load and latency with actual feeds before live operation; external caches decide when to refetch after an ERC-4906 event.

The shared-pool keeper discovers every on-chain group using `bucketCount()` and `bucketAt(index)`. There is no NFT list: one active pool containing 1,000 NFTs is maintained through one group operation. Configure `bucket_discovery.maximum_count` and `max_per_cycle` as reviewed inventory and scan bounds. A durable round-robin cursor advances before a live submission, so a busy first pool cannot starve the others. Scanning and RPC work grow with the number of groups rather than NFT supply; the current Vault caps all historical local groups at 16, including imported custody. Returning an existing bucket reuses its slot. The keeper also retains a separate defensive ceiling of 1,000 groups per cycle; that ceiling does not enlarge the contract limit.

`maintenance` supplies defaults for all discovered groups: `sync_external_rewards`, `minimum_liquidity`, `compound`, `minimum_compound_units`, `harvest`, `harvest_interval_seconds`, and reviewed `reward_routes`. Optional `bucket_overrides` uses decimal bucket IDs only to override these defaults; it does not limit discovery. The keeper verifies the group ABI, positive shares, pool occupancy, pool-key hash, and actual local assets from the pool registry before attempting work. It never trusts caller-supplied token lists or canonical home-token addresses for satellite compounding. External synchronization defaults to enabled and requires an explicit boolean. The keeper reads the receiver from Fees, validates fees/bucket bindings and its two-amount pending ABI, then targets Fees.syncExternal only when funded amounts are pending. It continues this bounded scan during pause and remote custody; while paused it suppresses all LP, bridge-reward and buyback operations. The existing nonce, signer, simulation, confirmation and budget guards apply unchanged. The example uses a minimal liquidity floor of 1; review per-pool bounds and raw-token compound thresholds for the deployment. On-chain oracle, price and slippage guards remain mandatory.

Buyback tasks supply a fee token, exact amount and direct approved pool ID. Historical funded satellite rewards can still be sent for Active, Outbound, Remote and Inbound groups, using operator-reviewed `reward_routes`; LP maintenance runs only for Active groups. Principal migrations and import acknowledgements remain deliberate reviewed operator calls. Discovery does not approve new routes or sign migration proposals.

Configurations containing the old `positions` NFT list are rejected. Persistent state carries `accounting_model=shared-pools-v2`; an older state file must be reviewed and deliberately migrated without deleting or bypassing any pending transaction. No automatic state reset is allowed.

For a reviewed test deployment, live mode additionally requires `--live`, reviewed runtime hashes for configured contracts, a matching encrypted `cast` wallet and private regular keystore/password files. Use a dedicated low-balance account with only keeper permissions. Do not use the governance wallet.

The program serializes transactions and writes intended nonce, destination, calldata and value durably before invoking the signer. Its pinned `cast send` call uses raw calldata as the positional argument, an explicit sender and a cleaned environment; the invalid old `--data` form has been removed. State replacement uses an exclusive random private temporary file and fsync rather than a predictable temporary path. A timeout/error leaves an ambiguous pending record. It must not be deleted just to force a retry.

## Deliberate migration procedure

1. Review compatible destination runtime code and custody/proxy authorities, economically equivalent tokens at identical decimals/nominal units, and actual provider funding/finality/retry behavior. A timelock notice is not proof of perpetual destination custody.
2. Onboard any missing immutable provider/channel policies and append-only principal, source-completion ACK, canonical-location ACK, and reward routes through each deployment's governance timelock. For a satellite B-to-C hop, register its remote-only policy and principal graph edge on the canonical chain too. Confirm wrapper/runtime/configuration pins, policy-to-route bindings, explicit source outbound enablement, and canonical token mappings across all participants before moving funds; module bindings and router endpoints are not replaced.
3. Schedule/execute the source governance proposal. Review the economic NFT, source pool, next custody epoch, immutable home identity, canonical asset mappings, and earliest execution time. Each move has an additional two-day migration delay; the keeper does not select a new chain autonomously.
4. A keeper calls `ZZZMigration.execute` after the delay, paying the bridge's native fee from operator funds. Watch source finality and provider status. No timeout permits a principal refund.
5. Verify destination `PrincipalAccepted`/`ImportPending`, the retained accepted-message snapshot, epoch, and exact token backing. A revisited satellite must have a drained old `Remote` record and accept a strictly newer local epoch. A keeper calls receiver `finalize` with fresh bounds; failed LP activation is retried without redirecting principal.
6. Call destination `acknowledge(migrationId, reverseRouteId)` for source completion. Confirm that the immediately previous source becomes `Remote` and its plan completes. When that source is the canonical home, this ACK also records the new home location.
7. For an onward hop between satellites, also call `acknowledgeHome(migrationId, homeRouteId)`. Verify canonical location events in strict next-epoch order. An out-of-order notification must be retried after earlier location updates; historical exact retries cannot regress the location. ACKs use the retained hop snapshot even if custody has since moved again.
8. On canonical return, location is recorded atomically during principal import; do not construct a self-chain home ACK. Send source completion to the satellite that released custody. No imported hop remints the NFT, creates another collective member or changes royalty weight.
9. Drain funded old remote reward budgets through their historical authorized routes, even after principal has left a source. The keeper attempts reviewed reward routes in Active, Outbound, Remote and Inbound states; it performs LP operations only in Active. Immutable NFT/token provenance selects the canonical asset; satellite dust joins the funded individual budget. Keep reward and principal reconciliation separate.

Source failure before successful submission reverts that transaction. Failure after a provider has taken custody requires its provider-specific recovery process, which is **not implemented by the generic router or adapter**. There is no safe catch-all admin recovery address. Real SDK wrappers and live bridging remain deferred until separately implemented, reviewed and staged.

This procedure covers equivalent-asset custody hops, not arbitrary asset conversion, moving the NFT off its canonical chain, or satellite holder top-ups. ## Incident and alert rehearsal

Before funded operation, configure and test alerts for keeper/indexer heartbeat, confirmed-block lag, deep reorg halts, oracle stale/down/recovery states, low keeper native balance, ambiguous nonce age, reverted maintenance, reward liabilities versus balances, principal/idle attribution, and source/destination migration epochs. Track both source-completion and canonical-location ACKs: a home location notification alone does not clear a pending source plan. Retry the correct retained ACK after verifying earlier epochs; do not edit on-chain locations or reset custody counters.

For an incident, preserve chain observations, logs and durable state first; stop the keeper signer if its key/RPC identity is uncertain. The guardian can pause strategy operations, but claims and authenticated inbound settlement remain available. Use timelocked governance for approved configuration changes. Rebuild the indexer from a verified deployment checkpoint and compare its ownership/event projection before resuming. Provider custody loss or unavailable delivery requires that provider's reviewed recovery protocol, which remains a release blocker here. Rehearse restoration and signer recovery on test infrastructure; these procedures have not been exercised on a funded deployment.
