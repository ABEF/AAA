# Creator-funded LP rewards

The creator can send payments directly to a bucket's dedicated `ZZZRewardReceiver` address. The creator does not need to enumerate NFTs or call a distributor. This is an implemented intake/accounting candidate, not a deployed payment address or proof that the creator will pay. No creator percentage or guaranteed reward entitlement is assumed.

## Payment and allocation

1. After sealing and activating a canonical bucket with positive shares, governance creates its immutable receiver through `FeeController.createRewardReceiver(bucketId, wrappedNative)`. After sealing this configuration call passes through the existing timelock. There is one receiver per bucket and at most 16 receivers; it is a helper created by the fee controller, not another sealed module.
2. The creator sends either original canonical pool ERC20 currency directly to that receiver. If `wrappedNative` is one of those admitted 18-decimal currencies, the receiver also accepts plain native ETH payments. Processing wraps that ETH one-for-one into the configured token. Native payments are optional; a zero wrapper creates an ERC20-only receiver.
3. Anyone may process pending payments using the receiver's `sync()` or `FeeController.syncExternal(bucketId)`. The keeper discovers the registered receiver from the fee controller, checks its bindings and `pending()` amounts, and submits the same constrained synchronization call. Ordinary receipt alone does not update NFT claim balances: funds become allocated when processed.
4. Measured funding enters the same 50/30/10/10 allocator as collected conventional LP fees. The 50% owner portion uses each NFT's nonredeemable shares in that bucket. The 30% collective portion uses lifetime contribution Power across all eligible canonical NFTs. The remaining two 10% portions reserve permanent compounding and approved buyback/burn funds. Each ten raw token units split five/three/one/one, with the existing bounded dust policy.
5. The current owner claims through `ZZZRewards.claim(nftId, token)`. Claim rights and already-funded unclaimed rewards follow NFT transfers. Wrapped native funding is claimed as its ERC20 wrapper; there is no native-ETH reward payout entrypoint. Dormant NFTs have zero shares and zero Power and receive no allocation.

For example, a processed $1,000 illustration reserves $500 for owners, $300 for collective rewards, $100 for compounding and $100 for buyback/burn. An NFT with 20% of bucket shares receives $100 from the owner portion. Its collective allocation depends separately on its share of total Power. Dollar amounts explain the policy; contracts account in exact token units rather than USD or expected yield.

`FeeController.totalExternalFunded(bucketId, token)` records actual processed external funding in raw units. Distinguish this from pending inbox balances, conventional collected fees, remote allocated/received counters, claimable liabilities and remaining compounding/buyback reserves. It does not measure all Pons revenue or an unpaid creator promise.

## Timing and migration

Allocation uses shares and Power when funding is processed. It does not reconstruct historical swap-time ownership or calculate a percentage of total Pons revenue. A payment arriving after another NFT has joined may benefit that NFT even if the creator earned it earlier. Payment eligibility, frequency and any attribution period require an explicit economic agreement and review.

Before any canonical activation/top-up changes shares or contribution Power, the vault processes **all registered inboxes** and settles their sub-ten-unit dust under the old membership. It repeats that global settlement after entry balancing and after the final LP mint/increase, because token and venue calls can deliver payments during entry. `syncAllExternal()` is vault-only; per-bucket processing remains permissionless. The barrier preserves the existing raw-unit dust exception, whose economic size must be reviewed for each admitted asset.

After the final LP update and global settlement, the vault pulls newly allocated compounding into attributed idle principal and adds that late value to incumbent NAV. Those funds cannot buy shares for the entrant. The vault then rechecks the 95% deployment requirement and rejects any remaining inbox payment or fee dust through `hasPendingExternal()`, including payments queued by a final token callback. Only after these checks does it checkpoint the entrant's owner rewards against the final old-share index, calculate new shares and increase contribution Power. A failing guard rolls back the whole entry. Late idle compounding cannot satisfy the $500 marginal **deployed LP** activation minimum.

A canonical receiver continues to allocate funded payments during a pause and while its physical LP is outbound, remote or inbound. Shares remain canonical and frozen while away. Owner/collective payments are funded directly at home and do not increment authenticated remote-liability counters or satisfy missing remote obligations. Home compounding reserves wait for compatible canonical maintenance after return. Strategy pauses continue to prevent LP maintenance, bridging and buybacks, while funded processing and claims remain available.

## Immutable custody and admission limits

The receiver permanently binds its fee controller, bucket, original canonical currency pair and optional wrapper. Governance cannot redirect received funds, change the receiver's bucket or replace the registered address. Only exact, reviewed token funding is credited. Plain transfers from anyone, including voluntary donations, are accepted revenue; ERC20 receipts do not prove the identity of a creator payment.

Use only the published receiver for the correct bucket and admitted currencies. Direct payments to the vault, rewards contract, fee controller or another inbox are not this funding path. Unsupported tokens sent to a receiver remain stranded. Constructor-time prefunding is quarantined and never allocated. With no native wrapper, ordinary ETH receipt reverts and forced ETH stays excluded from processing. There is no administrator rescue or sweep for these balances.

The receiver and admitted tokens/wrapper require independent review, including mutable token powers, blacklists, transfer taxes, rebasing, exact native wrapping and insolvency. An admitted token or wrapper that later stops working can prevent synchronization and therefore block membership changes across registered buckets. Runtime hashing alone does not prove immutable token/dependency behavior. The 16-receiver bound limits loops; it does not remove those admission and availability risks.

The production manifest declares `reward_model=external_funded_revenue`, which may supplement supported conventional fee collection. Its policy and exact-block receiver inventory must match [operating procedures](OPERATIONS.md). This payment intake does **not** add native-ETH liquidity settlement or support the selected Pons-hooked Uniswap pool; that separate adapter integration remains outstanding. External funding mode permits a reviewed zero-core-fee hookless ERC20 pool; conventional-fee-only mode still requires a positive core fee. The example 34% liquidity share is not a payout formula.
