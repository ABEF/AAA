# ZZZ Protocol — Full NFT Ownership, LP Accounting, Royalty Distribution & Production Readiness Audit

I want you to perform a **deep architectural review, security audit, implementation, and testing** of the entire NFT ownership and revenue distribution system.

This is a critical component of ZZZ Protocol. Take the time to understand the existing architecture, dependencies, interactions between contracts, possible vulnerabilities, and edge cases before modifying anything.

## 1. NFT Ownership, Liquidity Positions & Rarity Mapping

Consider this scenario:

**All 1,000 NFTs in the collection have been activated.**

This means there are 1,000 NFTs, each potentially owned by a different wallet, each representing economic rights to its associated permanently locked liquidity.

I need you to verify:

- Is every NFT uniquely identified by its `tokenId`?
- Is each NFT correctly mapped to its associated LP position or individually accounted liquidity?
- Is its rarity correctly associated with the same `tokenId`?
- Are the LP position, rarity, activation status, accumulated fees, and claimable rewards consistently associated with that NFT?
- If a liquidity position is rebalanced, replaced, or compounded, does its association with the NFT remain intact?
- Can any NFT accidentally receive another NFT's liquidity or rewards?
- Can all 1,000 NFTs maintain independent accounting and claim their rewards without gas scalability problems?

The system must use **NFT token IDs as the canonical identity for economic rights**, not wallet addresses.

Wallet addresses can change through NFT transfers, but the economic accounting must remain associated with the correct NFT.

One wallet may also own multiple NFTs. Each NFT must retain independent accounting.

## 2. NFT Transfers & Ownership of Unclaimed Rewards

This is especially important.

Imagine:

1. Wallet A owns NFT #123.
2. NFT #123 has a specific rarity and an associated permanently locked LP position.
3. The position generates $50 in LP fees and $20 in royalty rewards.
4. Wallet A has not claimed these $70.
5. Wallet A sells NFT #123 to Wallet B.

**Expected behavior:**

Wallet B becomes entitled to:

- All future LP fees generated by NFT #123's associated liquidity.
- All future royalty rewards attributed to NFT #123.
- All previously accrued but unclaimed LP fees ($50).
- All previously accrued but unclaimed royalty rewards ($20).
- All other unclaimed collective rewards associated with NFT #123.

Wallet A must immediately lose the ability to claim anything associated with NFT #123 after ownership transfers.

All unclaimed rewards follow the NFT, not its previous owner's address.

Verify and enforce this behavior across every relevant contract.

Cover secondary marketplace sales, direct ERC-721 transfers, transfers through approved operators, smart-contract wallets, multiple successive sales, and transfers while rewards are awaiting settlement.

Ensure that claims and ownership transfers cannot create double claims, reward theft, accounting corruption, or reentrancy vulnerabilities.

Explain how ownership is verified at claim time and ensure that the system remains correct regardless of transaction ordering.

## 3. Implement and Verify the Complete 80/10/5/5 Fee Distribution

The required distribution of trading fees is:

| Allocation | Percentage | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| NFT Owner | **80%** | Distributed to the owner of the NFT whose LP position generated the fees |
| Collective Reward Pool | **10%** | Shared equally among all active NFTs |
| Permanent LP Compounding | **5%** | Reinvested into permanently locked liquidity |
| ZZZ Buyback & Burn | **5%** | Used to buy and permanently burn ZZZ |

Every percentage must be implemented and enforced accurately.

### A. 80% — Individual NFT Owner

Each NFT receives 80% of the trading fees generated by its own associated LP position.

Verify that:

- Trading fees are correctly attributed to the originating NFT.
- Fees are isolated across NFT positions.
- Ownership changes are handled correctly.
- Unclaimed rewards remain associated with the NFT's token ID.
- Multiple claims cannot withdraw the same rewards.
- Fees are accounted for in their actual payment currencies.
- Accounting remains accurate after rebalancing and compounding.

### B. 10% — Collective Reward Pool

10% of trading fees enters the collective reward pool.

This pool must be **distributed equally among all active NFTs**, not weighted by rarity or LP size.

If 1,000 NFTs are active, each receives 1/1,000 of the distributable collective rewards.

Implement scalable accounting without iterating over all NFTs for every distribution or claim.

Handle activations and changes in the number of eligible NFTs over time. A newly activated NFT must not receive rewards generated before it became eligible.

Collective rewards already accrued to an NFT must remain claimable by its current owner, even after subsequent changes in activation status, subject to explicitly documented rules.

### C. 5% — Permanent LP Compounding

5% of collected trading fees must be reinvested into permanently locked liquidity.

Verify:

- How reinvestment is allocated across NFT-associated positions.
- That compounding cannot accidentally transfer value between NFTs.
- That rebalancing or changing an underlying LP position does not invalidate existing claims.
- That accumulated liquidity cannot be withdrawn through any unintended path.

Unless the existing specifications explicitly say otherwise, compounding should benefit the liquidity position that generated the fees.

### D. 5% — ZZZ Buyback & Burn

5% of trading fees must be allocated to purchasing and permanently burning ZZZ.

Verify:

- Correct fee allocation and custody.
- Appropriate swap routing, slippage limits, deadlines, and price validation.
- Protection against manipulation and MEV.
- That purchased ZZZ is actually burned and cannot be recovered.
- That failed swaps do not lose funds, corrupt accounting, or block unrelated reward claims.
- Secure retry and recovery mechanisms for temporarily unavailable liquidity or swap execution.

## 4. NFT Royalties — Separate Accounting & Rarity Weighting

In addition to LP trading fees, the protocol distributes NFT marketplace royalty revenue according to NFT rarity.

**Do not confuse royalty revenue with the 80/10/5/5 trading-fee distribution.**

These are separate revenue sources with potentially different distribution rules.

Verify:

- Where royalty revenue is actually collected.
- Which marketplace transactions generate royalties.
- Whether marketplace payments are guaranteed or voluntary.
- How collected royalties enter the protocol's reward accounting.
- How each NFT's rarity is stored and validated.
- How rarity influences the NFT's royalty entitlement.
- Whether royalty weights can change and how historical entitlements are preserved.
- How royalties are attributed, accumulated, claimed, and transferred with NFT ownership.

For example, a rare NFT may be entitled to a greater share of the royalty pool than a common NFT.

The weighting must be deterministic, transparent, and resistant to manipulation.

**Important:** ERC-2981 alone does not guarantee that marketplaces actually pay royalties. Verify real marketplace support, payment routing, and enforceability. Never account for hypothetical royalties as if they were received funds.

If the exact rarity weights or distribution parameters are missing from the specifications, identify that gap rather than silently inventing values.

## 5. Security, Failure Modes & Edge Cases

Perform a rigorous review of all components involved in NFT ownership, LP accounting, fee collection, reward distribution, compounding, and buyback execution.

At minimum, test:

- All 1,000 NFTs activated and independently claimable.
- Zero, one, or many active NFTs.
- Multiple NFTs owned by the same wallet.
- NFT transfers before or after fee accrual.
- NFT transfers before or after claims.
- Multiple transfers without intermediate claims.
- Transfers involving unclaimed LP, royalty, and collective rewards.
- Simultaneous claims and ownership-transfer transactions.
- Rapid activation and deactivation.
- Zero-fee periods and LP positions out of range.
- Rebalancing while fees are unclaimed.
- Multiple reward tokens and differing token decimals.
- Rounding errors, dust, precision loss, and accounting overflows.
- Insufficient balances and failed token transfers.
- Fee-on-transfer, rebasing, or otherwise nonstandard tokens where relevant.
- Failed buybacks, failed compounding, and unavailable price feeds.
- Interrupted keeper execution and repeated or delayed operations.
- Reentrancy, unauthorized withdrawals, claim duplication, and privilege escalation.
- Malicious ERC-721 receivers and smart-contract interactions.
- Incorrect rarity assignments or attempts to manipulate weights.
- Emergency pause, recovery, and restart procedures.
- Contract upgrades, migrations, and preservation of historical economic rights.

Pay particular attention to whether any administrator, keeper, upgrade mechanism, or migration pathway can bypass the permanent liquidity lock.

The permanent lock is a fundamental protocol invariant.

## 6. Architecture, Scalability & Gas Efficiency

The system should remain practical with 1,000 active NFTs and be extensible beyond that number.

Review the design for:

- O(1) or otherwise bounded-cost reward claims.
- Accumulator-based collective reward distribution.
- Rarity-weighted royalty distribution without unbounded loops.
- Efficient reward checkpoints and accounting updates.
- Secure token ID to liquidity position relationships.
- Accurate tracking of position changes across rebalancing operations.
- Batch claims where safe and economically practical.
- Protection against rounding-based value extraction.

Prove that the sum of individual claims, collective rewards, compounding allocations, buyback allocations, and remaining accounted balances never exceeds the actual fees received.

Identify every accounting invariant and enforce it with automated tests.
